## Follow-up post: the architecture

AI adoption is often discussed as a model-selection problem:

“Should we use ChatGPT or Claude?”

For many small businesses, that is not the first architectural question.

The more important questions are:

- Where does operational truth live?
- Which record is authoritative?
- Does every active client have a next action, owner, and date?
- What may AI retrieve?
- What may it change?
- Which actions still require a person?
- Can the business switch AI providers without losing its operating memory?

The architecture I recommend is provider-neutral:

```text
Human owner
→ ChatGPT and/or Claude
→ Google Drive, Sheets, Gmail, and Calendar
→ human approval
→ controlled action
```

Later, if the business outgrows the lightweight layer:

```text
AI interface
→ controlled API or MCP tools
→ PostgreSQL
→ Google Drive documents
```

The AI provider is the interface. It should not become the database.

## Follow-up post: CRM decision

A CRM is not bad.

Buying one before understanding the workflow is bad architecture.

Before selecting software, ask:

1. What decisions must the business support?
2. Which records are needed?
3. Where should each fact live?
4. Who owns each record?
5. Which workflows repeat?
6. What should AI support?
7. What must remain human-controlled?
8. What pain remains after a lightweight pilot?

Then choose:

- keep Google Workspace;
- add a CRM;
- add automation;
- add Postgres;
- build controlled MCP tools;
- build a custom application.

The technology should follow operating evidence.
